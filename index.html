Privacy statement

Instructor Toolkit for Canvas (instructor beta). Last updated 1 October 2026.

Where the Toolkit runs

The Toolkit runs inside your own Canvas session in your browser. Every request about your courses goes to the Canvas site you are signed into (for the USF pilot, usflearn.instructure.com) and uses your existing Canvas login. The one other address it reads is the public USF academic calendar, described below. It does not ask for or store a Canvas access token. With Load pages before you click on, Chrome may fetch, and for a few heavy pages fully load, a Canvas page your pointer rests on before you click it; those requests go to Canvas on your session like any other, Canvas may record a page view for a page you did not open, and nothing from such a page is stored.

What is not done with your information

Student information is not sold. No advertising is used. No third party analytics or tracking is used. The Toolkit does not send student work, comments, grades, or names to external AI systems or to any service outside your Canvas instance. Canvas information is not stored on an external Toolkit server, because there is no Toolkit server.

What is stored in your browser

Your preferences (which tools are on, default times, keyboard shortcuts, accent styling, the courses you chose to count), your Dashboard course groups (group names, course ids, which groups are collapsed, the order of cards inside each group, and the instructor names, cohorts, and labels you typed onto cards), your quick comment snippets, your announcement and message templates, your notes, self-reminders, and saved links, the Operations Check rules or issues you chose to ignore, the setup checklist items you marked complete (item id and date), and your personal activity targets are stored by Chrome in this browser's extension storage. Quick comment placeholders such as a student's name are filled in only at the moment you insert a comment and are never stored. Ignore entries contain course, rule, and item identifiers only. Collected teaching activity is kept for up to a day so the view opens again without re-reading Canvas; when you look at another instructor in a course, that cached record is about them (counts, Canvas ids, and timestamps of what they wrote in Canvas, never their message text) and it is held in this browser only. Backup and restore, on the Settings page, writes the sections you choose to one plain text file (.json) in your Downloads folder and reads such a file back. The file holds what the Toolkit stores about you (your settings, groups, snippets, templates, notes, checklist and ignore choices, and the dates you were in Canvas) and, only if you tick it, the follow-up memory's course and student ids; it never holds Canvas data, and the Toolkit keeps no copy of it.

Two small stores hold student ids without names, in this browser only. The Missing Work follow-up memory keeps a course id, a student id, the date, and how you made contact (a Toolkit message or Mark contacted) for sixty days, never the message or the name; you can clear any record. The figures collected for My Teaching Activity (counts and timings with Canvas ids, never text or names) are kept for a day for a finished period and two hours for the current one, so the view does not re-read Canvas each time.

A compact copy of each course's dataset (assignments, the roster with names and sections, and submission records reduced to ids, dates, states, and scores, with no comment text or submission content) is kept in session storage for up to an hour so the Toolkit views open at once after you move between pages (after ten minutes it is shown as the last read while a fresh one is read from Canvas), and is cleared when Chrome closes. The same session storage keeps the last read of your To Do list's grading counts, your course list, and each course's assignments and quizzes with their dates (override student ids only, no names) and its modules, for up to an hour, so the Dashboard and the Modules page open at once with what they showed last time while a fresh read runs; a save clears that course's copies.

A grading queue you start from the Grading view (course, assignment, and student ids with short labels) is kept in session storage for the current browser session so SpeedGrader can follow it, and is cleared when Chrome closes or when the queue completes.

If you mark a submission comment as reviewed in the comment inbox, that mark is kept in session storage for the current browser session and cleared when Chrome closes. It contains identifiers only, never comment text.

Find a course, on Canvas's All Courses page, reads the teachers of each of your courses from Canvas and keeps each course id with its teachers' names and Canvas ids in session storage for up to an hour, so the names appear at once when you come back to the page. It holds instructor names only, nothing about students, and is cleared when Chrome closes. What you type in its search box is kept for that tab only and is never sent anywhere.

When you open the Toolkit's toolbar button over a Canvas course, it reads that tab's address to learn which course you mean, and asks that Canvas page for what you choose: the course name, how many submissions are waiting, the roster file, the final grades file, the class list for Find a student (names and sections), or the email addresses you copy; it reads no other tab. What the button is given stays in it only while it is open; addresses you copy go to your clipboard, and a roster or final grades file you download, there or from the People page, the Gradebook page, or the Toolkit header, is written as a file to your Downloads folder. The Toolkit keeps none of it. Final grades export reads every student's course totals as Canvas computes them (current and final score, letter grades when the course has a grading scheme, unposted totals, and any final grade override) together with the roster, and writes them to that file; nothing is stored. An assignment's export reads that assignment's submissions with their rubric scores and comments, with the roster, and writes them to an .xlsx file the same way. Module Progress reads each student's module states from Canvas, one student at a time, holds them in the page's memory while the view is open, and writes them to an .xlsx file only when you ask. Groups (.csv) on the People page reads a group set's membership from Canvas's own export and writes it as a file. The Student Snapshot's access report reads, only when you click for it, which course content one student opened and when, exactly as Canvas's own Access Report page shows it, and writes it to a file only when you ask. The People page tools act on the page you are looking at and ask Canvas for nothing. The Toolkit side panel reads, for the course on screen and only when you use it, exactly what the toolbar button reads, in the same way. Quick search reads your course list from Canvas when it opens and searches the course content the session already holds (or reads it when you ask, as Search course content does); what you type stays in the box. The appearance choices (theme, hidden Canvas parts, page background) are preferences kept with your other settings; they change how Canvas looks in this browser and read nothing. Notes written in the side panel are the Notes view's notes, kept in this browser's extension storage as before; where the Toolkit tab sits, and whether it is docked in Canvas's navigation, is a preference kept with your other settings. Find a course in the toolbar button opens Canvas's All Courses page with your words in its address.

The Photo Roster shows the profile pictures students uploaded to Canvas, loaded from Canvas while the view is open, with their names, pronouns, and sections. Nothing from it is stored; a printed sheet exists only as the page you print.

Search course content reads a course's pages, assignments, quizzes, discussion and announcement opening posts, modules, file names, and syllabus from Canvas, and keeps that text in session storage for one course at a time, for up to six hours, so the search opens at once when you return; it is cleared when Chrome closes. It is the course's own content, not student work or student records. What you type stays in the Canvas page's address and is never sent anywhere else. The Upcoming due dates banner reads the assignments due soon in the courses you count and keeps their names, due dates, and published state in session storage for an hour; that is course content, not student work, and it is cleared when Chrome closes.

Everything else the Toolkit displays from Canvas (comments, submission content, health reports) is held in the page's memory while the tab is open and is not written to storage.

What the Toolkit can change in Canvas

The Toolkit changes Canvas data only when you take an explicit action: saving a date, renaming an assignment, quiz, or graded discussion in place, confirming a reviewed list of date changes, applying a labeled fix on the Operations Check, creating a rubric from a CSV you chose, posting a reply you typed, saving a grade you entered on a discussion post, saving the quiz question scores and comments you entered in the Quiz Bulk Grader, posting a comment on the whole quiz you typed there (a comment on the quiz's submission), writing the grades listed on a Bulk grade preview you confirmed (or undoing that run), leaving the comments listed on a Bulk comment preview you confirmed, recording the score you chose on missing work you confirmed (or undoing that run), marking an announcement as read for yourself, applying quiz accommodations or due-date extensions you previewed and confirmed, creating an unpublished copy of an announcement you chose, saving the announcement fields you edited in place, deleting or re-authoring an announcement you chose and confirmed by name, saving the quiz review settings (what students may see after submitting) you confirmed on a preview, or sending Inbox messages you reviewed and confirmed one by one. It never changes grades, enrollments, or content on its own. For the Days logged into Canvas figure it also keeps, in this browser, a list of the dates on which it saw you in Canvas: dates only, nothing about what you opened. Slim view on the Modules page is likewise a display choice kept in this browser; it changes nothing in Canvas and nothing students see. The only thing it deletes is an announcement you confirmed by name, and Canvas keeps deleted announcements on the course's Undelete page.

The USF academic calendar

For closed-day notices and grade deadline reminders, the Toolkit reads the public "Important Dates and Deadlines" page published by the USF Office of the Registrar (usf.edu/registrar/calendars) about once a week. That request carries no cookies, no Canvas information, and nothing about you or your students; it is the same page anyone can open in a browser. The parsed dates are kept in this browser's extension storage. Turn off the reminder switches in Settings and the page is never requested.

Diagnostics

When Developer diagnostics is turned on in Settings, the Toolkit prints timing and flow information to the browser console. Student names, IDs, submission content, comments, and grades are removed before anything is printed. Nothing is transmitted.

Removing the Toolkit

Removing the extension from Chrome deletes everything it stored: preferences, templates, notes, the follow-up memory, and the activity cache. Nothing else needs to be cleaned up because nothing is stored anywhere else.
